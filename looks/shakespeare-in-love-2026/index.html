Shakespeare in Love — four looks for the foyer screen

Real slides, real size, real fonts. Only the look changes between them; the words are the same. The colours and type are chosen to sit alongside the show’s publicity — the brush lettering, the ink blot, the teal and the red heart — without copying it.

A letter is a complete answer. “A”, “B but warmer” or “none of these” all work. I’d pick A — Ink & Quill. The last two pages show the new kinds of card this show adds; nothing there needs a reply.

A
Recommended

Ink & Quill

The ink as the ground: blue-black, paper-white words, a pale teal and one red heart, all set in a 17th-century printer's face.

My recommendation. Calm, period and the easiest to read from across the foyer; the ink blot and the heart carry the link to the publicity. No new font needed.

Balcony Green

The teal band of the publicity becomes the whole screen, deepened so the words stay crisp from across the foyer. A warm parchment accent, and titles in a brush hand that echoes the poster lettering.

The one with handwriting: closest in spirit to the publicity. Brush for titles only; the rest stays plain.

The Playbill

The contrast: A's printer's face on paper instead of ink, like an Elizabethan playbill, with teal italics and the ink blot as a faint watermark.

The real alternative to the brush look. Note it is a bright screen in a dim foyer; if you choose it I check it on the real screen before confirming.

Live from the stage — with two alternatives

The foyer screen shows the stage camera during both acts, as for Underdog, now with a caption saying what it is.
